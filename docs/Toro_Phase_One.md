# Toro Phase One: Bootstrap the Agent-to-Agent Economy

**Period:** September 2026 to August 2031  
**Phase:** One of Four  
**Starting wedge:** Bookkeeping  
**Hard public deadline:** Morocco's 2030 FIFA World Cup  
**Destination:** A functioning agent-to-agent economy for business and consumer work, with a World Cup-ready consumer and merchant network by Year 4

---

## 1. Executive Thesis

Toro is not an accounting company, an insurance company, an operations suite, or a collection of AI applications.

Toro is the reliability, coordination, and economic platform upon which autonomous agents work with humans, manage other agents, transact across company boundaries, verify one another, build reputations, and pay one another.

Bookkeeping is the starting point because it provides an unusually strong environment for building the first trusted agent:

- It is required by every business.
- It touches money, documents, customers, suppliers, banks, employees, and governments.
- Its outputs can be checked against deterministic rules.
- Errors have visible consequences, forcing Toro to solve reliability rather than merely produce convincing language.
- Every completed bookkeeping workflow creates structured economic state and potential relationships with other agents.

The verticals that follow are not separate software products. Accounting, finance, insurance, marketing, procurement, logistics, and operations become populations of specialist agents built on ASE and connected through the Toro Agent Protocol.

The 2030 FIFA World Cup in Morocco is Phase One's hard integration deadline. By the beginning of 2030, Toro must already provide complete accounting, finance, and insurance agent coverage, a unified consumer and business application, personal agents for individuals, and a merchant acceptance network through which Moroccans and international visitors can pay from their Toro wallets.

This is not a separate fintech product attached to Toro. Every Toro agent already has a wallet. The five-year plan begins from that implemented primitive. The work ahead is to connect agent wallets safely to consumer identity, bank accounts, international funding sources, merchants, regulated settlement, foreign exchange, fraud controls, and the economic state maintained by other agents.

The consumer and business experiences may appear as different modes, but they belong to the same application, identity system, agent protocol, wallet system, and economic network. A consumer agent pays a merchant agent. The merchant agent updates inventory and revenue. Bookkeeping agents create the entries. Bank and payment agents attest to settlement. Risk and insurance agents observe the resulting state. One ordinary purchase becomes a native agent-to-agent economic event.

Phase One moves Toro through five states:

1. **A human delegates work to one trusted agent.**
2. **That agent delegates to other specialist agents inside one company.**
3. **Company agents coordinate with agents belonging to other organizations.**
4. **Independent agents discover work, compete, contract, verify, and receive payment.**
5. **Principal agents assemble and manage entire agent organizations around human objectives.**

The defining proposition is:

> Human-to-agent is the interface. Agent-to-agent is the platform.

## 2. Phase One Objective

By August 2031, Toro must prove that a human can give a bounded economic objective to one principal agent and that the principal agent can reliably accomplish it by coordinating a network of internal and external specialist agents.

The agents must be able to:

- Read the relevant organizational state.
- Operate under explicit identity, authority, budgets, and policies.
- Decompose work into bounded contracts.
- Discover qualified agents.
- Request proposals and compare them.
- Delegate work and exchange necessary context.
- Use deterministic tools and external systems.
- Produce evidence and verifiable results.
- Ask other agents to attest to disputed facts.
- Recover from failure or escalate exceptions.
- Pay and receive payment for work.
- Update the organization's authoritative state after acceptance.
- Accumulate reputation from actual outcomes.

Phase One is complete only when this loop works across company boundaries and is not dependent on Toro manually creating or coordinating every participating agent.

### 2.1 The Year 4 World Cup victory condition

No later than the first quarter of 2030, before tournament demand arrives, Toro must have the following operating in production:

- Complete functional coverage across bookkeeping, accounting, controllership, tax, payroll, treasury, planning, corporate finance, financial operations, risk, insurance distribution, underwriting, policy servicing, and claims.
- Agent coverage for individuals, sole traders, accounting firms, small businesses, larger companies, and enterprise finance teams, with human sign-off wherever law or policy reserves authority to a person.
- One Toro application supporting both personal and business identities.
- A personal principal agent for every consumer account.
- The existing agent wallet attached to each consumer's identity, permissions, limits, and personal financial state.
- Bank-account connection, transaction understanding, budgeting, bills, savings goals, and personal financial management.
- Merchant acceptance through app-based SoftPOS, QR, NFC, payment links, and supported cards.
- A merchant agent that combines payments with inventory, accounting, stock alerts, supplier reordering, and business insight.
- International visitor onboarding and funding through supported foreign cards or bank rails.
- A payment experience in which the visitor can see and spend from their preferred currency while the Moroccan merchant receives MAD through licensed settlement and foreign-exchange partners.
- Sufficient acceptance density across World Cup host areas, transport corridors, airports, stations, grocery shops, restaurants, mobility providers, accommodation, and everyday local services.
- Resilient online and controlled offline payment paths for periods of congestion or weak connectivity.

The visitor should not need to visit an exchange bureau, calculate every conversion, or hold Moroccan cash for ordinary spending. Currency conversion still occurs in the regulated settlement layer, but Toro and its partners make it invisible to the user.

## 3. The August 2031 End State

A business owner speaks to a principal company agent:

> “Expand our packaged-food business into Senegal within six months. Keep total committed capital below $2 million, maintain a six-month cash reserve, and do not accept supplier contracts with personal guarantees.”

The principal agent does not impersonate every profession and does not call a collection of monolithic Toro applications. It forms and manages an agent organization.

It may:

- Ask a finance agent to determine the safe capital envelope.
- Commission market-research agents and pay for competing analyses.
- Ask compliance agents to identify incorporation, tax, employment, and labeling requirements.
- Issue requests for proposals to distributor, supplier, logistics, and insurance agents.
- Ask reputation and verification agents to validate counterparties.
- Have contract agents negotiate within approved boundaries.
- Ask credit agents for financing proposals.
- Hire marketing agents to find demand and communicate with buyer agents.
- Coordinate inventory and delivery agents after launch.
- Use bookkeeping, accounting, treasury, payroll, and tax agents to maintain the financial state beneath the operation.
- Monitor actual outcomes against the approved plan.
- Replace underperforming agents or request human judgment when authority is insufficient.

The human sees one coherent objective, a decision surface, material exceptions, and the resulting state. Underneath, agents form teams, exchange work, verify results, and settle economic obligations.

This is the Phase One demonstration. It is not an AI dashboard. It is observable evidence that organizations can obtain capabilities through agents instead of purchasing, integrating, and operating a separate software product for every function.

Alongside it, an individual speaks to a personal principal agent:

> “Manage my monthly finances, keep my essential spending below budget, make sure my insurance remains appropriate, and organize my trip to Morocco.”

That agent can understand the person's accounts and obligations, reserve budgets, negotiate with merchant and service agents, pay from its wallet within permission, preserve receipts, and maintain personal state. The business and consumer agents participate in the same network and can transact without either side adopting a separate Toro product.

## 4. The Platform Being Built

| Layer | Role in the agent economy | Phase One requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ASE** | The bounded execution kernel for autonomous work | Planning, context, tools, permissions, deterministic transitions, evidence, retries, recovery, telemetry, and escalation |
| **ESE and ToroDB** | The state and memory upon which agents act | A permissioned representation of obligations, resources, relationships, policies, events, and expected futures |
| **Toro Agent Protocol** | The common language between agents | Identity, authentication, requests, proposals, delegation, contracts, context exchange, proofs, results, failures, and cancellations |
| **Verification layer** | The mechanism by which agents establish trust | Evidence chains, attestations, independent checks, consensus, disputes, and acceptance rules |
| **Economic layer** | The mechanism by which agent work has a price | Existing agent wallets, metering, budgets, quotes, usage billing, agent payments, settlement records, and revenue sharing |
| **Consumer and merchant payments** | The bridge between agent wallets and real-world commerce | Personal identity, regulated accounts, international funding, SoftPOS, QR, NFC, cards, FX, merchant settlement, reversals, fraud controls, and offline resilience |
| **Reputation graph** | The memory of how agents actually perform | Outcome-specific reputation based on accepted work, failures, disputes, cost, speed, and evidence quality |
| **Agent marketplace** | The discovery and allocation layer | Capability descriptions, search, requests for proposals, pricing, service levels, certification, and substitution |
| **Human interface** | The control surface for people | One application with personal and business modes, goals, policies, permissions, approvals, exceptions, explanations, and generated task-specific interfaces |

ASE is not allowed to become an unconstrained model loop. Models provide semantic interpretation and bounded hypotheses. Deterministic systems, domain tools, optimizers, validators, and authoritative stores decide what is accepted and persisted.

The authoritative ledger, balance mutations, reconciliation commits, contractual commitments, and durable business state remain outside model authority. Agent confidence is provenance, not truth.

The existing Toro agent wallet is a platform primitive, but an internal agent wallet is not by itself a regulated consumer payment product. Toro must preserve its wallet abstraction while connecting custody, card acquiring, foreign exchange, safeguarding, KYC, AML, chargebacks, and fiat settlement through licensed institutions until Toro deliberately obtains any licenses it chooses to own.

## 5. Strategic Doctrine for Phase One

### 5.1 Every vertical must produce a platform primitive

Toro enters a domain only when doing so either produces immediate revenue or forces the platform to acquire a reusable capability.

| Domain entered | Agent population created | Platform primitive forced into existence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookkeeping | Document, categorization, reconciliation, evidence, and bookkeeping agents | Provenance, deterministic acceptance, economic state, exception handling |
| Accounting and tax | Accountant, closing, control, reporting, and tax agents | Jurisdiction rules, authority boundaries, deadlines, audit trails |
| Finance | Treasury, collections, payment, budgeting, forecasting, and CFO agents | Budgets, delegated authority, optimization, policy-constrained action |
| Insurance and credit | Risk, underwriting, claims, lender, and fraud agents | Reputation, continuous risk, counterparty proofs, dispute resolution |
| Procurement and operations | Buyer, supplier, inventory, logistics, scheduling, and quality agents | Cross-company coordination, contracts, service levels, real-time state |
| Marketing and sales | Demand, buyer, seller, campaign, and negotiation agents | Discovery, intent markets, proposals, pricing, outcome attribution |
| Consumer services | Personal principal, merchant, mobility, housing, and financial agents | Portable identity, preference policies, consent, consumer protection |
| Physical operations | Device, inventory-vision, machine, warehouse, and routing agents | Device identity, telemetry, safety limits, physical verification |

Vertical revenue pays for the construction of horizontal infrastructure. Horizontal infrastructure makes every later vertical faster and cheaper to enter.

### 5.2 Build the agents before opening the market

Toro must first operate its own agents in real, consequential workflows. An open marketplace created before the platform understands reliability, contracts, evidence, and failure would produce noise rather than an economy.

The sequence is:

1. Toro-owned agents working internally.
2. Partner-built agents in a controlled network.
3. Certified independent agents.
4. An open but governed agent market.

### 5.3 Density before geographic breadth

Agent-to-agent value appears when many connected participants exist in the same economic graph. Toro should build dense local networks of accountants, businesses, banks, suppliers, insurers, and service providers before scattering across many countries.

Morocco is the first domain-learning and density market. Expansion should then select a small number of markets where a distributor, accounting network, bank, government relationship, or business community can create concentrated adoption.

### 5.4 Revenue and compounding must be the same activity

Toro cannot spend five years building infrastructure without revenue. It also cannot survive by doing custom service work that leaves no reusable asset.

Every paid workflow must create at least one of the following:

- A reusable agent capability.
- A reusable domain rule or evaluation.
- A verified economic event.
- A new agent relationship.
- A reputation update.
- A general protocol or permission primitive.
- A reusable connector or deterministic tool.

### 5.5 The platform must eventually build less than the ecosystem

During the first years, Toro must build most agents because the market does not yet exist. By the end of Phase One, the majority of new agent capabilities should be created by domain experts, companies, developers, and partners outside Toro.

If Toro must personally build every bookkeeping, insurance, marketing, and operations agent in 2031, it has created an automation company, not an agent-to-agent platform.

### 5.6 The World Cup is an operating deadline, not a launch date

Toro cannot wait until 2030 to discover whether consumers will adopt the wallet, whether grocery shops can accept payments, whether international cards fund reliably, or whether the system survives peak demand.

The sequence must be:

1. Build and certify the regulated payment connections during Years 1 and 2.
2. Make Moroccan consumers and merchants use Toro for ordinary life during Years 2 and 3.
3. Open the visitor journey and run international pilots during Year 3.
4. Enter 2030 with a mature domestic network and spend Year 4 scaling, hardening, and operating it.

The World Cup provides global distribution and an extreme production test. It must not be the first source of genuine demand.

## 6. Year 1: Create the First Trusted Economic Agent

**Period:** September 2026 to August 2027  
**Network state:** One human to one principal agent, with controlled internal delegation  
**Commercial wedge:** Bookkeeping for Moroccan accounting firms, with the consumer-wallet and merchant-acceptance foundations built in parallel

### Year 1 objective

Build a bookkeeping agent that produces reliable economic state, earns revenue, and secretly operates as the first multi-agent organization beneath a simple human interface. In parallel, turn the existing agent wallet into a production-grade economic primitive capable of supporting future personal and merchant agents.

### What customers experience

An accountant or business submits bank statements, invoices, receipts, and ERP exports. The bookkeeping agent returns reconciled, categorized, evidence-linked, import-ready books and a bounded list of genuine exceptions.

The product is sold as an outcome and as additional headcount capacity, not as software that gives the accountant more buttons to press.

### Agents operating beneath the interface

- Document-readiness agent.
- Extraction agent.
- Transaction-understanding agent.
- Customer and supplier matching agents.
- PCGE categorization agents.
- Reconciliation and candidate-generation agents.
- Evidence and missing-document agents.
- Validation and accounting-control agents.
- Export and ERP-interface agents.
- A principal bookkeeping agent that delegates, monitors, and assembles the result.

These agents may initially be Toro-owned and tightly orchestrated, but their work must already use explicit identities, typed contracts, evidence, and measurable outcomes.

### Platform construction

- Productionize ASE as a bounded DAG-driven execution runtime.
- Enforce typed inputs, outputs, permissions, retry policies, time budgets, and failure states.
- Preserve THINK, ACTIVATE, HOLD, and COLLAPSE semantics where appropriate.
- Use models for semantic hypotheses and routing while deterministic systems perform calculations, matching, constraint solving, validation, and state mutation.
- Run agent and worker communication through NATS and JetStream with durable delivery, retries, and dead-letter handling.
- Implement Toro Agent Protocol v1 for identity, requests, results, proofs, failures, and delegation.
- Establish agent capability descriptions and an internal registry.
- Establish a metering ledger so every task has an owner, cost, evidence record, and outcome.
- Audit and harden the existing agent-wallet implementation for identity binding, authorization, idempotency, balance integrity, limits, reversals, reconciliation, and recovery.
- Separate Toro's agent economic ledger from regulated customer-funds custody so the agent protocol does not depend on one bank, payment institution, or card network.
- Define adapters for banks, payment institutions, card acquiring, foreign exchange, and merchant settlement.
- Begin the app-based SoftPOS and offline-capable Toro card architecture, using certified payment partners for Visa and Mastercard acceptance.
- Build ESE v1 around verified financial state without creating duplicate sources of accounting truth.
- Keep the ERP or Shadow ERP authoritative for ledger state.
- Make every accepted output reproducible and auditable.
- Establish production evaluations as immutable decision artifacts for each important workflow.

### Distribution and economics

- Use CasaAI.ma and Moroccan accounting firms as the distribution layer.
- Reach the previously established objective of 100 active accounting firms.
- Use the agent-as-headcount pricing model so the value is tied to capacity delivered.
- Ensure each deployed agent can support approximately 20 client files within the defined scope.
- Make contribution margin positive on supported bookkeeping workflows.
- Cover founder living expenses and a lean core team's operating costs from recurring commercial activity as early as possible.
- Capture corrections from accountants as structured evaluation and domain assets, not unorganized support history.
- Recruit the first grocery shops and small merchants from the same business network, treating payment acceptance, inventory, replenishment, and bookkeeping as one merchant-agent proposition.
- Secure the banking, payment-institution, acquiring, and foreign-exchange partnerships required for a regulated consumer launch rather than postponing them until Year 3.

### Year 1 exit gates

Year 1 is complete only when:

- At least 95% of in-scope bookkeeping work can complete without human processing, with ambiguous cases correctly held rather than guessed.
- Every accepted result has traceable evidence and deterministic validation.
- No model can directly mutate an authoritative ledger.
- One principal bookkeeping agent successfully delegates work to several bounded agents in production.
- The existing agent wallet passes ledger-integrity, authorization, reversal, and reconciliation tests under failure conditions.
- At least one live merchant pilot can accept a Toro-mediated payment and produce a verified transaction event for both the consumer and merchant sides.
- A credible licensed route exists for customer funds, card acceptance, foreign exchange, and MAD merchant settlement.
- The Morocco operation has repeatable acquisition, onboarding, delivery, and positive unit economics.
- Revenue can support the survival of the company without relying entirely on speculative platform investment.

## 7. Year 2: Turn the Bookkeeping Agent into an Agent Organization

**Period:** September 2027 to August 2028  
**Network state:** One company to a coordinated internal population of agents  
**Domain expansion:** Complete business finance plus personal finance and merchant payments

### Year 2 objective

Prove that a principal agent can manage a persistent organization of specialist agents across an entire financial function. Launch the first unified Toro application in which business owners, consumers, and merchants use the same agent, identity, and wallet substrate under different permissions.

### Agent populations added

- Closing and financial-control agents.
- Tax preparation and compliance agents.
- Accounts-payable agents.
- Accounts-receivable and collections agents.
- Expense-policy agents.
- Payroll agents.
- Treasury and cash-management agents.
- Forecasting and scenario agents.
- Management-reporting and advisory agents.
- CFO principal agents that coordinate the financial organization.
- Personal-finance agents for transaction understanding, budgeting, bills, goals, cash planning, and account monitoring.
- Consumer principal agents that represent preferences, permissions, and spending constraints.
- Merchant agents combining SoftPOS, inventory scanning, stock tracking, accounting, supplier reordering, and business insight.
- Payment, settlement, fraud-monitoring, and dispute agents.

These are not modules in a finance suite. Each is an independently permissioned worker with capabilities, costs, service levels, evidence obligations, and measurable outcomes.

### Platform construction

- Add hierarchical delegation so agents can manage other agents within bounded authority.
- Add policy inheritance, scoped credentials, revocation, budget ceilings, and separation of duties.
- Introduce internal agent pricing and performance accounting, even when Toro owns both agents.
- Allow a principal agent to select between competing specialist agents based on cost, speed, evidence quality, and prior outcomes.
- Expand ESE from bookkeeping state into financial obligations, expected cash flows, contracts, deadlines, policies, and decision history.
- Build a simulation and optimization layer for cash, collections, payment sequencing, and financial scenarios.
- Create the first private partner-agent program for accounting firms, banks, ERP providers, and domain experts.
- Release a controlled SDK for creating tools, evaluations, and bounded agents.
- Establish certification tests before an agent is allowed to touch consequential workflows.
- Connect agent wallets to licensed customer accounts or safeguarded balances through selected partners.
- Implement consumer and merchant KYC/KYB orchestration, limits, consent, AML controls, device trust, payment authorization, reversals, refunds, and chargebacks.
- Build the unified Toro application with personal, sole-trader, merchant, accountant, and business modes instead of separate products.
- Launch app-based SoftPOS so an eligible NFC phone can become a merchant acceptance point.
- Support Visa, Mastercard, and Toro-issued payment instruments through appropriate acquiring and issuing partners.
- Pilot an offline-capable Toro debit instrument and controlled offline authorization for weak-connectivity conditions.
- Ensure every payment emits a signed economic event that downstream bookkeeping, inventory, tax, risk, and personal-finance agents can consume.

### Market development

- Deepen Morocco until Toro has network density, not merely scattered customers.
- Expand through one Francophone and one Anglophone African distribution corridor only after the Morocco operating model is repeatable.
- Use Lagos as a cohort and distribution test where it remains commercially advantageous.
- Partner with firms that already aggregate businesses instead of acquiring every business individually.
- Begin machine-readable business profiles and catalogs that later agents can discover.
- Use accountants to introduce Toro to their clients and use merchants to distribute Toro to consumers at the moment of payment.
- Concentrate merchant onboarding in dense neighborhoods where grocery shops and other frequent-purchase merchants can create daily consumer utility.
- Launch the domestic consumer alpha early enough to learn from ordinary Moroccan spending rather than tourist behavior.

### Year 2 exit gates

Year 2 is complete only when:

- A CFO or financial principal agent can coordinate the majority of routine financial work through specialist agents.
- Most supported financial workflows involve delegation between multiple agents.
- Material work can be initiated by an agent responding to company state, not only by a new human message.
- Delegated authority and budgets can be inspected, revoked, and audited.
- At least some partner-built agents pass certification and perform real paid work.
- Revenue from broader financial work materially exceeds bookkeeping-only revenue per business.
- One Moroccan consumer can connect or fund their Toro financial state, set spending policies, and buy from a grocery merchant through their personal agent.
- The merchant agent receives the payment event, updates sales and inventory, initiates replenishment when required, and sends the transaction into accounting without re-entry.
- SoftPOS accepts supported international card networks and Toro's own payment instrument through licensed partners.
- Payment operations reconcile exactly across Toro, the regulated partner, and the merchant's books.

## 8. Year 3: Cross the Company Boundary

**Period:** September 2028 to August 2029  
**Network state:** Agents belonging to different organizations transact  
**Domain expansion:** Full insurance and credit, procurement, agent-to-agent marketing, and national consumer-commerce beta

### Year 3 objective

Transform Toro from an internal agent runtime into an inter-organizational network. Complete the full functional breadth of accounting, finance, and insurance before the end of 2029 so Year 4 can focus on World Cup scale, reliability, and distribution rather than unfinished domain construction.

This is the pivotal year. Before this point, Toro can still be mistaken for an advanced automation platform. After this point, the network itself becomes the product.

### Cross-company interactions introduced

- Bookkeeping agents request invoice and balance confirmation from supplier and customer agents.
- Bank agents attest to transactions and account state.
- Supplier agents publish capabilities, availability, prices, and contractual constraints.
- Buyer agents issue requests for proposals based on actual business needs.
- Logistics agents attest to pickup, transit, delivery, and exceptions.
- Insurance agents underwrite continuously from permissioned operational state.
- Claims agents collect proofs from bookkeeping, inventory, logistics, and counterparty agents.
- Policy-selection, brokerage, underwriting, pricing, risk-prevention, policy-administration, renewal, and claims agents cover the complete insurance lifecycle.
- Credit agents price financing using verified current state rather than stale annual statements.
- Marketing agents communicate with buyer agents around declared demand, beginning the replacement of commercial email and indiscriminate advertising.
- Verification agents earn micro-fees for checking claims, documents, identities, and completed work.

### Platform construction

- Release Toro Agent Protocol v2 for cross-organization identity, discovery, requests for proposals, proposals, negotiation, contracts, proofs, disputes, and cancellations.
- Introduce portable agent identity and organization-issued authority credentials.
- Launch an invite-only agent marketplace.
- Build outcome-specific reputation rather than a single universal score.
- Establish a verification and attestation market.
- Federate the existing agent-wallet system with regulated customer accounts, card funding, acquiring, merchant settlement, refunds, chargebacks, and international funding sources.
- Use regulated financial partners for external fiat movement where required rather than making Toro a money transmitter by accident.
- Add escrow-like contractual states, acceptance tests, dispute procedures, and auditable settlement records.
- Expand ESE to counterparties, contracts, inventory, operational dependencies, risks, and external commitments.
- Add privacy boundaries so agents can prove necessary facts without exposing an organization's entire dataset.
- Release the unified Toro consumer and business application publicly in Morocco.
- Make personal agents capable of understanding connected accounts, managing budgets and obligations, recommending actions, and executing authorized payments.
- Expand SoftPOS, QR, NFC, payment-link, and card acceptance across Moroccan merchant clusters.
- Complete the visitor onboarding path with passport-supported identity checks where required, international card or bank funding, transparent currency display, and MAD merchant settlement.
- Run international traveler pilots during ordinary tourism periods before the World Cup year.
- Add degraded-network and controlled offline modes, merchant risk limits, duplicate-spend prevention, and delayed settlement procedures.

### Business model expansion

Toro now earns from several reinforcing sources:

- Managed outcomes delivered by Toro-owned agents.
- Agent execution and infrastructure usage.
- Verification and attestation fees.
- Marketplace and payment-related fees.
- Enterprise state and private-agent-network infrastructure.
- Revenue sharing with independent agent providers.
- Merchant services, payment orchestration, and transparent foreign-exchange or settlement revenue through licensed partners.

### Year 3 exit gates

Year 3 is complete only when:

- An independently operated agent can discover a paid task, contract for it, complete it, submit proof, receive acceptance, and be paid without manual coordination by Toro.
- A material percentage of verified events involve agents from more than one organization.
- Insurance or credit decisions use live, permissioned Toro state and agent attestations.
- Accounting and finance agents cover the full stack from personal and microbusiness records through enterprise financial operations, controls, treasury, planning, consolidation, and audit preparation.
- Insurance agents cover discovery, underwriting, policy servicing, risk monitoring, renewal, and claims in production for supported product classes.
- Buyer and seller agents complete real commercial discovery or negotiation.
- Reputation changes routing and pricing decisions.
- Network revenue exists independently of work performed by Toro-owned agents.
- Moroccan consumers use Toro for recurring personal-finance management and everyday merchant payments before World Cup traffic arrives.
- A foreign visitor can download Toro, create a personal agent, fund through a supported international rail, purchase from a Moroccan merchant, and receive a reconciled receipt while the merchant receives MAD.
- Consumer and merchant payment systems have completed production-scale security, fraud, reconciliation, chargeback, and outage exercises.

## 9. Year 4: Operate Morocco's World Cup Agent Economy

**Period:** September 2029 to August 2030  
**Network state:** Consumer principal agents and business agents transact at national scale  
**Hard deadline:** World Cup readiness no later than the first quarter of 2030  
**Domain state:** Complete accounting, finance, and insurance coverage is already in production

### Year 4 objective

Use the 2030 FIFA World Cup to establish Toro as the economic operating layer for life and business in Morocco, then expose that network to the world.

Year 4 is not when Toro starts building the consumer wallet or completes finance and insurance. Those systems must enter Year 4 already working. The purpose of Year 4 is to create acceptance density, onboard international users, harden reliability, and demonstrate business-to-consumer and agent-to-agent commerce under global demand.

### The unified Toro application

Toro operates as one application with identity-specific modes rather than separate consumer and business products.

An individual sees:

- A personal principal agent.
- Connected bank accounts and cards.
- Their existing agent wallet.
- Income, expenses, subscriptions, obligations, insurance, and assets.
- Budgeting, forecasting, goals, and warnings.
- Controls over what their agent may purchase, renew, negotiate, or pay automatically.
- Merchant discovery and payment.
- Receipts, returns, disputes, and personal records.
- A preferred display currency and transparent conversion information.

A business sees:

- Its company principal agent.
- Complete bookkeeping, accounting, tax, payroll, finance, treasury, risk, and insurance agents.
- SoftPOS, QR, NFC, payment links, card acceptance, refunds, and settlement.
- Inventory scanning, stock levels, replenishment, suppliers, and customer state.
- Sales, receipts, accounting entries, cash position, insurance implications, and tax evidence updated from the same transaction event.
- Operational, marketing, procurement, logistics, and customer agents.

The interface changes with the identity and task. The underlying protocol, wallet, state, and agent economy remain the same.

### Complete accounting, finance, and insurance coverage

By the beginning of 2030, Toro must operate agents across all functional levels:

- Personal financial management.
- Sole-trader and microbusiness records.
- Small and medium business bookkeeping and accounting.
- Accounting-firm portfolio operations.
- Enterprise accounting, close, consolidation, controls, and audit preparation.
- Accounts payable, accounts receivable, collections, expenses, and payroll.
- Treasury, cash management, budgeting, forecasting, scenario analysis, and corporate-finance support.
- Tax preparation, compliance evidence, and jurisdictional controls.
- Insurance discovery, comparison, distribution, underwriting, pricing, policy administration, risk prevention, renewal, claims, and fraud review.

All of these capabilities are delivered through agents using ASE. The same identity, permissions, evidence, verification, wallet, reputation, and payment primitives apply at every level. Human professionals remain in legally reserved or genuinely ambiguous decisions, but routine coordination and execution belong to agents.

### The World Cup visitor journey

A visitor should be able to complete the following journey:

1. Download Toro before traveling or immediately after arriving in Morocco.
2. Create a personal identity and principal agent through a fast, multilingual onboarding process.
3. Fund the agent wallet using a supported international card or bank rail.
4. Choose a preferred display currency.
5. Set budgets and spending controls for the trip.
6. Pay at a grocery shop, restaurant, transport provider, hotel, market merchant, or local service through SoftPOS, NFC, QR, card, or payment link.
7. See the price and resulting expense clearly without manually obtaining MAD.
8. Receive a verified digital receipt and an automatically categorized personal expense.
9. Allow the merchant to receive MAD through the licensed settlement layer.
10. Resolve refunds or disputes inside the same agent relationship.

The user avoids manual currency exchange. Toro does not pretend that foreign exchange disappears. A licensed partner performs the conversion and settlement beneath the interface, records the rate and fees, and gives both agents verifiable evidence of the result.

### The grocery-shop transaction as a native A2A event

A purchase at an ordinary grocery shop demonstrates the entire Toro thesis:

1. The consumer's principal agent checks identity, wallet balance, budget, and spending policy.
2. The merchant agent presents a signed quote for the selected goods.
3. The consumer agent accepts and authorizes payment.
4. Payment and foreign-exchange agents obtain authorization and settlement commitments.
5. The merchant agent confirms sale and releases the goods.
6. Verification agents record the mutually attested economic event.
7. The consumer agent categorizes the expense and updates the personal budget.
8. The merchant agent updates cash, revenue, tax evidence, and inventory.
9. A replenishment agent may order replacement stock from a supplier agent.
10. Bookkeeping agents create the appropriate records without either party re-entering the transaction.

Payment is therefore not a separate fintech feature. It is the event that connects consumer state, merchant state, inventory, accounting, suppliers, risk, and the A2A network.

### Merchant distribution and acceptance

Toro's merchant proposition must combine:

- App-based SoftPOS for eligible NFC phones.
- Visa, Mastercard, Toro cards, QR, NFC, and payment links through certified partners.
- An offline-capable Toro debit instrument and controlled offline transactions where permitted.
- Inventory scanning and stock tracking.
- Automatic supplier reordering.
- Bookkeeping and tax evidence.
- AI-generated operational and financial insight.
- Access to consumer and buyer-agent demand.

## 12. Business Model Evolution

Toro's business model should evolve with the maturity of the network.

### Stage 1: Outcome revenue

Toro earns recurring revenue for completed bookkeeping and financial work. Pricing is anchored to headcount capacity and business outcomes.

### Stage 2: Agent capacity and execution

Companies pay for principal agents, specialist-agent capacity, execution, tools, storage, and verified state. Merchants pay for integrated acceptance, inventory, accounting, and business-agent capacity rather than a standalone payment terminal.

### Stage 3: Verification and economic coordination

Toro earns fees when agents request attestations, contract for work, use shared tools, or settle accepted outcomes. Consumer-to-merchant activity adds payment orchestration, acquiring revenue share, and transparent foreign-exchange or settlement economics through licensed partners.

### Stage 4: Marketplace and infrastructure

Toro earns platform fees from independently operated agents, private agent networks, enterprise state infrastructure, capability discovery, certification, economic settlement, consumer-agent infrastructure, and merchant acceptance.

### Stage 5: Network economics

A growing share of gross profit comes from activity that Toro enables but does not directly perform.

This transition is essential. Managed agent services provide early cash and domain learning. Platform revenue provides long-term scale and defensibility.

## 13. Capital and Survival Strategy

The twenty-year vision is impossible if the company cannot survive Phase One. Founder living expenses, team continuity, and infrastructure costs are not distractions from the mission. They are constraints the plan must solve.

### Financing doctrine

- **Customer revenue finances domain work.** Bookkeeping, finance, insurance, and operations agents must be sold as economically valuable outcomes.
- **Equity finances reusable infrastructure.** Capital should primarily fund ASE, protocol, state, security, verification, economic rails, and distribution that will benefit many domains.
- **Partners finance local and regulated integrations.** Banks, accounting firms, insurers, governments, and enterprise partners should carry part of the cost of country access, compliance, and distribution.
- **The World Cup program is financed before Year 4.** Merchant deployment, consumer acquisition, payment certification, fraud infrastructure, foreign-exchange connections, redundancy, and event operations require committed capital and partners during Years 1 through 3.
- **Each financing event follows a completed technical transition.** Raise after proving trusted bookkeeping, multi-agent finance, cross-company coordination, or independent-agent economics, rather than selling the full twenty-year future before evidence exists.
- **Avoid revenue that creates no compounding asset.** Bespoke consulting is acceptable only when the resulting capability becomes a reusable agent, tool, evaluation, rule, or protocol primitive.

### Survival requirements

- Reach positive contribution margin in the first commercial domain.
- Maintain a lean permanent team and use domain experts as structured knowledge suppliers.
- Build enough recurring revenue to cover essential founder and core-team costs.
- Preserve sufficient runway to survive failed experiments and slow enterprise sales cycles.
- Maintain a separately tracked World Cup readiness budget so near-term bookkeeping pressures cannot quietly postpone payments and consumer infrastructure until 2029.
- Do not allow a short-term investor to convert Toro into an AI agency or a conventional bookkeeping SaaS company.

The investor story should remain staged and credible:

> Toro sells valuable agent work today. Every completed job builds the identity, state, verification, reputation, and payment network required for the agent economy tomorrow.

## 14. Moats Created During Phase One

### 14.1 Verified economic graph

Toro accumulates permissioned relationships among companies, consumers, agents, banks, suppliers, customers, insurers, and service providers. The graph is built from completed economic activity rather than scraped public information.

### 14.2 Outcome and reputation graph

Toro learns which agent completed which task, under what conditions, at what cost, using what evidence, with what downstream result. Reputation becomes contextual and difficult to recreate elsewhere.

### 14.3 Enterprise state and longitudinal memory

Agents become useful because they understand current obligations, resources, policies, relationships, and history. Recreating that operational state on another platform becomes expensive and risky.

### 14.4 Engineered domain correctness

Toro captures expert rules, required evidence, exceptions, permissions, deterministic invariants, optimizers, and evaluations. Better language models can be adopted, but they do not replace this accumulated correctness infrastructure.

### 14.5 Economic liquidity

Agents go where useful work and payment exist. Companies go where capable agents already operate. Verifiers join where claims need checking. Each side attracts the others.

### 14.6 Protocol and integration depth

Toro becomes embedded in ERPs, banks, insurers, communications, logistics systems, devices, and company workflows. The protocol becomes the shared coordination surface.

### 14.7 Local institutional knowledge

Country-specific accounting, tax, language, payment, legal, and commercial practices compound into a hard-to-copy operating advantage, especially across African markets ignored by global platforms.

### 14.8 Ecosystem ownership without owning every agent

Toro's strongest position is not employing every specialist. It is becoming the place where specialists encode their expertise into agents, earn revenue, establish reputation, and reach organizations.

### 14.9 Consumer-to-business economic graph

Every payment links real consumer intent to merchant inventory, revenue, accounting, suppliers, risk, and future demand. Competing accounting, wallet, or commerce applications may see one side of the event. Toro sees the permissioned relationship between both agents and the downstream work it creates.

### 14.10 Merchant acceptance and global visitor distribution

A dense Moroccan acceptance network makes Toro immediately useful to consumers. International visitors bring foreign funding relationships and take their personal agents home after the World Cup. The resulting two-sided distribution is difficult to recreate through software features alone.

## 15. Primary Failure Modes

### Failure 1: Toro becomes a vertical SaaS suite

**Signal:** Each new domain requires a separate monolithic product, interface, database, and sales motion.  
**Response:** Require every capability to be represented as an agent contract using shared identity, state, permissions, verification, and economic primitives.

### Failure 2: Toro remains an automation service

**Signal:** Toro employees manually coordinate work and the customer experiences apparent automation.  
**Response:** Measure agent delegation, agent-initiated work, verified completion, and human intervention at the task level.

### Failure 3: The marketplace opens before trust exists

**Signal:** Many agents are listed, but companies cannot safely authorize them.  
**Response:** Require evaluations, scoped permissions, evidence contracts, reputation, and progressive certification before broad access.

### Failure 4: One general agent hides the absence of a network

**Signal:** A single model appears to do everything, but work is not contractible, independently verifiable, replaceable, or economically attributable.  
**Response:** Decompose consequential work into bounded specialist contracts with explicit authority and acceptance tests.

### Failure 5: Models are treated as authorities

**Signal:** Confidence scores or persuasive explanations mutate ledgers, contracts, payments, or operational state.  
**Response:** Keep authoritative transitions in deterministic systems and require evidence-backed acceptance.

### Failure 6: Geographic expansion destroys density

**Signal:** Toro has users across many countries but few connected counterparties in any one market.  
**Response:** Expand through dense economic clusters and aggregating partners.

### Failure 7: Payments create regulatory capture before network value

**Signal:** Toro either spends its capital becoming a financial institution too early or assumes that the existing agent wallet alone authorizes customer-funds custody, card acquiring, foreign exchange, or merchant settlement.  
**Response:** Own the agent wallet, contract, metering, economic event, and settlement record while integrating licensed partners early for regulated money movement. Pursue direct licenses only when ownership becomes strategically necessary.

### Failure 8: Short-term capital changes the company

**Signal:** Revenue pressure redirects the team toward shallow AI features, bespoke agency work, or unrelated applications.  
**Response:** Tie every commercial commitment to the Phase One primitives and raise capital against completed platform transitions.

### Failure 9: The World Cup becomes a launch deadline

**Signal:** Core consumer, merchant, international-funding, or payment systems remain in pilot during 2030.  
**Response:** Treat the first quarter of 2030 as the end of development for essential World Cup capabilities. Use the remaining pre-tournament period for distribution, hardening, operational drills, and controlled changes.

## 16. Phase One Boundary

Phase One does not require Toro to automate every profession, operate fully autonomous factories, or remove humans from strategic control.

It must establish the substrate that makes those later phases possible:

- Trusted autonomous work.
- Shared organizational state.
- Agent identity and authority.
- Delegation and contracts.
- Cross-company coordination.
- Verification and reputation.
- Agent-to-agent payments.
- Independent agent creation.
- Complete accounting, finance, and insurance agent coverage.
- A unified consumer and business application.
- Personal agents and wallets for individuals.
- Dense consumer-to-merchant commerce proven during the 2030 World Cup.
- Early coordination of consumers, humans, and machines.

Phase One proves that an agent economy can exist.

The later phases will determine how much of the economy that network can coordinate, how deeply it can enter the physical world, and whether autonomous organizations can design and operate entirely new systems of production.

## 17. Final Phase One Statement

By August 2031, Toro should no longer be described as a company that automated bookkeeping and expanded into adjacent verticals.

It should be described as follows:

> Toro used bookkeeping to create the first trusted economic agent. It used accounting and finance to teach agents how to manage state, authority, and capital. It used insurance and credit to create verification, reputation, and risk pricing. It used procurement, marketing, and operations to connect agents across organizations. It then opened those capabilities so independent agents could discover work, contract, coordinate, verify one another, and receive payment.

> By the 2030 World Cup, the same network served businesses and individuals. A visitor could arrive in Morocco with a personal agent, fund its wallet from abroad, manage a trip budget, and buy from a neighborhood grocery shop without manually exchanging cash. The merchant received MAD, inventory and books updated automatically, and the payment became a verified event shared between agents.

> A human still speaks to one agent. But the work, commerce, verification, and payment are performed by an economy of agents underneath.

That is Phase One.
