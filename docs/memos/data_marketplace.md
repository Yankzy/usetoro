# Internal Memo: The Data Marketplace Strategy

## Executive Summary

We are not building bookkeeping software.

We are using bookkeeping to bootstrap a Data Marketplace: a network where organizations publish authenticated business events through autonomous agents and are compensated whenever those events create value.

Today, businesses spend enormous effort reconstructing reality from documents, PDFs, emails, bank statements, and spreadsheets. Our long-term objective is to eliminate reconstruction entirely. Instead of asking AI to infer what happened, we ask the participants who created the event to attest to it.

Bookkeeping is simply the first application built on this marketplace because it has an immediate customer with a measurable return on investment.

---

# The Problem

Today's software ecosystem is document-centric.

A bookkeeping system asks for:

* Bank statements
* Invoices
* Receipts
* Utility bills
* Payment confirmations

AI then attempts to infer reality from incomplete evidence.

This approach is fundamentally limited.

Instead, the participants that created these events already know the truth.

The bank knows the payment occurred.

The utility company knows the bill was paid.

The merchant knows what was purchased.

The shipping company knows when the package arrived.

The tax authority knows whether the filing was accepted.

Our platform allows each participant to expose an autonomous agent that can publish authenticated business events.

The marketplace buys these events whenever they are needed.

---

# Vision

Build the world's largest marketplace for authenticated business data.

Participants are no longer API providers.

They become suppliers of verified business events.

Consumers are AI agents performing work.

Instead of exchanging documents, agents exchange authenticated facts.

---

# Core Principle

Every verified business event has economic value.

If an organization provides a trustworthy answer that enables another organization to automate work, that organization should be compensated.

Examples include:

* Payment confirmation
* Invoice confirmation
* Delivery confirmation
* Utility payment confirmation
* Telecom payment confirmation
* Tax filing confirmation
* Identity confirmation
* Payroll confirmation

Every confirmation becomes a micro-transaction.

# Revenue Model

Customers do not purchase raw data.

Customers purchase completed work.

For example:

An accountant pays for automated bookkeeping.

Behind the scenes, our platform purchases authenticated business events from marketplace participants.

Revenue flows like this:

Accountant

↓

Our Platform

↓

Marketplace Participants

Each participant receives micro-payments proportional to the value of the information they contribute.

Our platform retains a marketplace fee.

---

# Why This Works

Traditional APIs are treated as infrastructure costs.

Our marketplace transforms them into revenue-generating assets.

Instead of paying to maintain an API, organizations earn income whenever their information is used.

This aligns incentives.

More automation creates more demand for authenticated business events.

More demand increases marketplace revenue.

Objective:

---

## Phase Two

Introduce authenticated verification.

Instead of asking users for documents, the bookkeeping agent requests confirmations from marketplace participants.

Examples:

Request payment confirmation from bank agent.

Request invoice confirmation from supplier.

Request utility payment confirmation from electricity provider.

Initially, these confirmations may coexist with traditional uploads.

---

## Phase Three

Build the marketplace.

Organizations deploy autonomous agents.

Each agent connects internally to existing systems.

They do not need to expose new APIs.

The marketplace standardizes communication.

Participants begin earning recurring revenue from verification requests.

# Why Bookkeeping Comes First

Bookkeeping has three important properties.

First, the customer already exists.

Accountants have an immediate pain point.

Second, the return on investment is measurable.

Saving hours of bookkeeping justifies payment immediately.

Third, bookkeeping naturally requires trusted information from many external organizations.

Every accounting workflow increases demand for authenticated business events.

Accounting therefore becomes the engine that finances marketplace growth.

---

# Long-Term Network Effect

Every new participant increases the value of every existing participant.

More banks increase accounting accuracy.

More merchants improve categorization.

More utilities reduce manual work.

More accountants generate more marketplace demand.

The marketplace compounds.

Eventually, applications are no longer the primary asset.

The authenticated business event network becomes the company's core competitive advantage.

---

# Guiding Principle

We are not building software that reads business documents.

We are building the infrastructure through which autonomous organizations exchange authenticated business events.

Bookkeeping is our first product.

The Data Marketplace is the company.
