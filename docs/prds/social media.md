# Product Requirements Vision (PRV)

## Vision

The product is a mobile-first social world that combines live human interaction, spatial discovery, creator-driven experiences, and AI-powered utility inside a persistent digital city. The platform is not a VR metaverse, and it is not a traditional social media feed. Instead of scrolling endlessly through disconnected content, users move through live spaces where people gather around experiences, personalities, events, entertainment, and services.

The core insight behind the product is that humans are naturally drawn toward places where activity is happening. Modern social media flattened social interaction into timelines and recommendation feeds. This platform restores the feeling of wandering through living environments where people gather, perform, socialize, debate, flirt, compete, and discover things together in real time.

The product behaves less like a social network and more like a persistent digital city powered by human social energy and AI-assisted experiences.

AI is an enabling layer throughout the platform, but it is not the center of the social experience. Human beings remain the primary source of social value, emotional unpredictability, entertainment, attraction, and cultural momentum. AI exists to create atmosphere, utility, continuity, and interaction depth.

The long-term goal of the platform is to create the first truly socially alive digital environment optimized for mobile devices.

---

# Core Product Philosophy

The platform is built around the idea that places are more emotionally meaningful than feeds.

Users do not open the app and consume isolated posts. Instead, they enter a world where activity is spatially organized. Concerts happen in clubs. Debates happen in arenas. Comedy happens on stages. Sports fans gather in bars and stadiums. Service providers host offices. Friends naturally encounter each other while moving through districts.

The world itself acts as the discovery interface.

The product intentionally avoids the mistakes of previous metaverse attempts. Users are not expected to wear VR headsets or navigate realistic simulations. The experience is lightweight, stylized, fast, and emotionally immersive rather than graphically immersive.

The interface must feel accessible to users who have never played video games before.

# Product Structure

The platform is composed of persistent places connected through navigable districts.

A place is the fundamental unit of the product. Every meaningful interaction happens inside a place. Examples include comedy clubs, rooftop music venues, chess cafés, AI accountant offices, sports bars, debate stages, fashion lounges, podcast rooms, arcades, startup hubs, and social plazas.

Each place contains atmosphere, occupants, activities, and interaction mechanics.

Atmosphere includes music, lighting, visual identity, crowd density, sound effects, animations, and environmental styling. The goal of atmosphere is not realism but emotional context.

Occupants include both humans and AI entities. Humans are socially central. AI entities serve role-based functions such as DJs, bartenders, accountants, commentators, tutors, shopkeepers, chess opponents, and game masters.

Activities define what users actually do inside the place. Activities may include spectating performances, voice conversations, dancing, debates, shopping, live events, games, collaborative experiences, or AI-assisted services.

Places persist even when users leave. Users can return to familiar locations, continue unfinished activities, revisit favorite venues, and build routines around recurring spaces.

---

# Human Social Layer

The product is fundamentally social. The most important metric in the platform is not content volume but social density.

Users should constantly feel the presence of other people nearby. Crowds, movement, voice chatter, reactions, and visible activity are critical to the experience.

Human gatherings are intentionally distinguishable from AI-driven interactions. When users see crowds gathering around an event, they understand that real people are present there. This creates emotional authenticity and trust.

The platform is designed to support both active and passive social participation. Users may choose to perform, socialize, spectate, flirt, wander, or simply exist in social environments without pressure to constantly produce content.

Voice communication is a foundational interaction layer. Users can naturally approach each other and begin conversations without leaving the world or opening external interfaces. When two users intentionally engage, the system transitions into a focused social interaction state where ambient noise softens and voice becomes clearer and more intimate.

The platform is expected to naturally generate friendship networks, recurring communities, and dating behavior through repeated social proximity and shared experiences.

---

# Hot Zones and Social Discovery

One of the platform’s defining systems is the hot zone system.

Hot zones are dynamic areas of collective attention that emerge when users gather around events, creators, performances, or social activity. These zones become visually and audibly noticeable from nearby locations through environmental effects such as glowing lights, music, crowd noise, visual pulses, reactions, movement patterns, and atmosphere changes.

Users discover activity spatially rather than algorithmically.

Examples of hot zones include live concerts, comedy performances, sports watch parties, public debates, celebrity appearances, AI-hosted events, dance floors, or spontaneous social gatherings.

Hot zones create urgency and curiosity because they communicate that something is actively happening right now. The system transforms collective attention into visible geography.

The platform should encourage users to drift toward activity naturally rather than rely entirely on feeds or recommendation tabs.

# Creator Economy

Content creators are one of the primary pillars of the platform.

Creators do not simply upload content. They host experiences.

A comedian may host a live rooftop stand-up performance. A musician may run a nightclub event. A streamer may host a sports commentary arena. A debate host may run political discussions. A fashion creator may organize social lounges. A chess expert may operate a strategy café.

Creators become destinations inside the world.

This changes the relationship between audiences and creators from passive consumption into live social participation. Audiences gather around creators in real time, generating visible crowd energy and collective atmosphere.

Creators monetize through multiple systems.

The first monetization layer is direct audience support. Users may send gifts, reactions, visual effects, tips, applause, or premium interactions during live experiences.

The second monetization layer is ticketed access. Creators may host private events, VIP sessions, exclusive performances, tournaments, workshops, or premium social gatherings with controlled entry.

The third monetization layer is persistent venue ownership. Popular creators may establish recognizable spaces that users repeatedly visit. The value of these venues comes from social gravity rather than artificial scarcity.

The fourth monetization layer is sponsorship and brand partnerships integrated directly into experiences rather than through traditional advertisements.

In the future, creators may optionally train AI extensions of themselves that maintain presence while they are offline. These AI extensions may greet visitors, answer common questions, maintain atmosphere, or continue lightweight interactions without replacing the creator’s primary live presence.

---

# AI Services and Utility

AI plays a supporting but important role throughout the world.

Users may interact with AI accountants, AI tutors, AI DJs, AI bartenders, AI therapists, AI companions, AI commentators, AI chess opponents, AI storytellers, and AI game masters.

AI interactions are spatially contextualized. Instead of opening isolated chat interfaces, users visit places where these services exist.

For example, a user may visit an accountant’s office for financial guidance, enter a chess café to continue a long-running game against an AI opponent, or visit a debate hall moderated by an AI host.

AI interactions are persistent when appropriate. Users may maintain ongoing relationships, unfinished games, recurring conversations, or long-term progress over time.

The world itself is not fully AI-generated. Environments are primarily hand-crafted and modular to ensure performance, consistency, and cost efficiency. AI is selectively used where intelligence, unpredictability, memory, or dynamic interaction creates meaningful value.

---

# Exploration and Immersion

The platform operates through two primary interaction states.

The first state is exploration mode. In exploration mode, users wander through the world, discover events, encounter people, notice hot zones, and move between places. Exploration mode is lightweight and optimized for speed and accessibility.

The second state is immersive mode. When users enter an experience, the application transitions into a focused interaction layer. A concert may become a full-screen live performance experience. A debate may become an interactive audience session. A chess café may transition into a playable game interface. A social conversation may transition into focused voice communication.

This layered approach allows the product to maintain both large-scale social discovery and deep engagement experiences simultaneously.

---

# Technical Architecture

The platform consists of three major technical systems.

The world simulation layer manages districts, locations, occupancy, navigation, environmental state, and persistent spaces.

The realtime social layer manages user presence, spatial audio, proximity interactions, crowd synchronization, and live events.

The AI interaction layer manages conversational NPCs, AI-powered activities, moderation assistance, event orchestration, memory systems, and dynamic interactions.

The rendering layer prioritizes lightweight stylized visuals optimized for mobile devices. The system is designed for high interaction density rather than high graphical fidelity.

---

# Monetization

The monetization strategy combines cosmetics, creator economy systems, premium AI interactions, and branded experiences.

Users may purchase outfits, accessories, animations, apartment customizations, effects, emotes, venue styling, and identity cosmetics.

Creators monetize through tips, subscriptions, ticketed events, venue ownership, and sponsorships.

AI services operate on a metered model where lightweight interactions remain inexpensive while premium long-memory or advanced reasoning interactions are usage-based.

Brands may establish persistent experiential locations inside the world such as sports arenas, technology showrooms, music events, educational hubs, or interactive retail environments.

---

# Strategic Goal

The strategic goal of the platform is to create a persistent social environment that feels alive every time a user enters it.

The platform should create the feeling that culture is happening spatially around the user in real time. Users return not only because there is new content, but because there are familiar places, recurring people, live events, evolving social energy, and ongoing relationships.

The platform succeeds if users begin to think of it not as an app they consume, but as a place they visit.
